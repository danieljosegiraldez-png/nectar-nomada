# Yeast and microbial starter cultures in coffee fermentation: literature catalog

Dated 2026-10-06.

## Method and honesty notes

- Searches and page reads used WebSearch and WebFetch only. No paywall, captcha or access-denied page was bypassed. Skipped for that reason: sbicafe.ufv.br (HTTP 403, Anubis block; three UFV records not read) and one PMC article, PMC10937909 (captcha page; it appears to be a 2024 paper on yeast mutant strains for arabica fermentation and was not read, so it is not in the catalog). One further PMC hit (PMC11405557) was not opened.
- Every field below comes from what the fetched page showed. WebFetch returns a model-written summary of the page, not the raw text. I did not read any full paper end to end, and several entries rest on abstract or repository metadata only. Treat the table as a screening catalog and check dose, temperature and sensory details in the original paper before design decisions.
- "not stated" means the fetched content did not give it. Nothing was filled from memory.
- Limitations column: where the source gave no limitations, that is written, followed by limits that are visible from the study design as retrieved (for example, single cultivar or country).
- Honey-process studies: none found. Costa Rica, Panama: no inoculation study found. Vietnam: one (S24). Ecuador: one diversity study without inoculation (S26). The Frontiers 2024 mini-review on Coffea canephora (Bernardes et al., https://frontiersin.org/journals/food-science-and-technology/articles/10.3389/frfst.2024.1377226/full) lists no Costa Rica, Panama, Vietnam, Ecuador or Colombia starter studies in its Table 2, which agrees with this gap.
- Organism coverage: Lachancea, and Pichia kluyveri in a peer-reviewed coffee-bean study with full detail, were not found at usable detail (P. kluyveri appears in S6, S15, S16, S26 and the patent S27). Hanseniaspora appears in S5, S15, S20.

## Handling and behavior patterns the evidence supports

Each statement uses only the listed studies. Where one study cannot carry a claim alone, that is said.

### Starter dose and form
- Cell densities in the studies that stated them cluster between about 10^7 and 10^8 cells per mL or per g of coffee: 10^7 to 10^8 per mL (S7), about 10^7 per g (S8, S10), about 10^8 per mL (S5), 3 x 10^7 per mL (S19), 10^9 per mL on sterilized beans (S3). S4 reports about 7 log CFU/mL. A starter reached about 10.0 log CFU/mL vs 8.0 in spontaneous fermentation when started near 10^8 (S5). No study here tests dose against cup score with a replicated design. The only dose comparison is in the patent (S27: higher isoamyl acetate at 5 x 10^7 CFU/g in the Brazil example), which is not independent evidence. Vietnam's dose comparison (S24) is confounded with pectinase.
- Applying the starter in a bucket or tank outscored spraying in two studies on natural-process cherries: S. cerevisiae in buckets (S2) and SafAle S-33 in bucket vs spray (S13). In S2, C. parapsilosis did better with direct application, so the best method depends on the strain.

### Temperature
- Reported fermentation temperatures were mostly ambient: about 18-21 C (S7), 22-26 C (S8), 25-27 C (S19), 13-25 C (S17), 20 +/- 1 C (S25), 25 C (S22). Starter cultures were grown at 28 C or 30 C (S3, S7, S10, S15, S18) or 35 C for LAB (S11).
- Temperature is confounded with oxygen in the only studies that report it across lots: aerobic lots averaged 36.26 C vs 28-31 C anaerobic (S20), and semi-anaerobic whole-fruit lots reached 37 C vs about 20 C in SIAF (S25). In both, the warmer open lots differed in pH, moisture and color (S20) or had more acceptable-or-poor samples and faster embryo deterioration beyond 72 h (S25). Neither study varied temperature on its own, so a temperature effect itself is not shown.

### pH, Brix and acid trajectory
- A controlled wet fermentation with a yeast plus LAB starter went from pH 4.47 to 4.05 and from 15.8 to 8.45 degrees Brix over 36 h, with titratable acidity of 42.69 mg/g (S11). That is one lot and one study.
- In S3 (sterilized beans), bacterial treatments formed only malic, lactic and acetic acids, while yeasts formed higher alcohols and aromatics. In S8, malic acid fell by 75.39% in co-cultures within 36 h. In S10, organic acids rose with fermentation time (0 to 96 h).
- Starter did not always acidify: on green beans, pH generally rose after yeast fermentation except with the Sourvisiae strain (S9). That setup is not a cherry fermentation, so it should not be applied to cherry or parchment lots.
- pH was measured in S17, S18, S19, S20 and S25 but the retrieved text did not give trajectories. Do not assume a target pH from these.

### Duration
- Durations tested ranged from 12 h to 5 days: 12 h best for yeast count and titratable acidity (S19, 48 h run); 18 h giving an 84-point lot (S12); 24 h (S22); 36 h (S5, S11); 48 h (S15, S17); 72 h (S4, S8, S9, S16, S20); 100 h (S14); 5 days (S18); 180 h (S7).
- In the one study that varied time for the same starter, T. delbrueckii on natural coffee gave honey and almond notes at 0-48 h and fruity and winey notes at 72-96 h; volatile levels fell as time lengthened while sensory scores improved (S10). So volatile totals did not track the cup in that study.
- In spontaneous SIAF, shorter times scored better and quality-rated samples decreased with longer time (S25). Beyond 96 h the authors call the effects understudied (S25). Not tested with a starter.

### Cup quality and process type
- Inoculated lots scored 80 or more in several studies: all lots above 80 and 86.50 for T. delbrueckii in pulped (S7); fine conilon 80.0-89.0 with L. mesenteroides (S8); 84.50 (S11); 84 (S12); 82.00 for a W. anomalus mutant vs 75.50 uninoculated (S17); 81.65 for P. fermentans on Robusta (S18). Robusta in Vietnam reached 71.8 (S24).
- Panels were small or unreported in most of these: three evaluators in S5; single Q-grader not clearly replicated in S13; panel size not stated in the others. The gains are therefore suggestive, not firm.
- Higher scores were not universal: L. plantarum alone reached 79.9 (S16). On green beans, commercial wine and beer yeasts raised floral and fruity notes but lowered overall liking among 98 consumers (S9).
- Anaerobic or SIAF lots with starters: S7, S8 and S11 report scores above 80, but S7 lacks a stated uninoculated SIAF comparison, and S11 as retrieved has none.

### Strain and organism pattern
- Strain choice mattered by process: T. delbrueckii best in pulped, C. parapsilosis best in natural (S7); L. mesenteroides outperformed the yeasts in conilon SIAF (S8); P. fermentans beat E. faecium in Robusta (S18).
- Starters were reported to persist or dominate in some studies (P. fermentans over native microbiota, S1; stable low-diversity community, S22). Others tracked the inoculant by qPCR (S2, S7, S8).
- No Lachancea study was found. Hanseniaspora data are limited to S5 (with P. kudriavzevii, lab scale), S15 (screening) and S20 (physicochemical only). Wickerhamomyces data come from Thailand and Indonesia and are cup-score or volatile based (S17, S19).

### Risks and off-flavors
- Seed viability fell in self-induced anaerobic lots with yeast but not in conventional natural drying (S7). Spontaneous SIAF kept embryo viability above 80% to 120 h, while semi-anaerobic lots deteriorated faster after 72 h (S25). These two results are not directly comparable (S7 inoculated vs a sun-dried control; S25 spontaneous, two oxygen regimes). Whether the starter causes viability loss is not settled.
- Ochratoxin A was measured only once, in S11, where none was detected. Fungal suppression was reported in S6 (M. caribbica cut filamentous fungi 63-90%) and S23 (C. krusei inhibited A. ochraceus on assay, with higher cup scores). S22 states it did no mycotoxin analysis. Taken together, evidence of ochratoxin reduction in real lots is thin.
- B. subtilis alone produced guaiacol (S3); the paper proposes it as a flavor marker, and the retrieved text does not call it a defect.
- Klebsiella sp. is used in two Indonesian studies (S21, S22) without any safety data in the retrieved text.
- Lower overall liking in S9 shows that more aromatic complexity can score lower with consumers than a control, at least for green-bean fermentation.

## Where results conflict or do not line up

- Viability: S7 (SIAF plus yeast lowered seed viability vs sun-dried natural) vs S25 (SIAF protected viability vs semi-anaerobic). Different comparators.
- Starter value: S5 and S17 report higher scores for inoculated lots, S9 reports lower liking, S16 stays below 80 and S7 reports no sensory effect of inoculation on beverage quality as the authors put it.
- Volatiles vs cup: S10 shows volatile levels falling while sensory improved; S19 reports volatile gains without any cup score. Volatile totals are a weak guide to cup.
- Best inoculation route depends on strain: S2 (bucket best for S. cerevisiae, direct best for C. parapsilosis).
- pH: S11 acidified; S9 pH rose on green beans.
- S25 summaries disagreed on whether sensory quality differed across lots; resolve in the paper.

## Gaps in the record
- Honey process, Costa Rica, Panama: no study found. Ecuador: diversity only (S26). Vietnam: one dry-process Robusta study at 71.8 points (S24).
- Lachancea: no coffee study found. P. kluyveri: tested in S6 (Brazil, natural, conilon) and S16 (Colombia), and in the patent S27; no peer-reviewed arabica wet-process trial with full conditions was retrieved.
- Pichia kluyveri patent (S27) and two theses or student works (S9, S16) are not peer-reviewed journal articles.
- Dose, temperature, duration, replicate counts, panel size and cup-score baselines were often missing from the retrieved text; those cells say "not stated".
