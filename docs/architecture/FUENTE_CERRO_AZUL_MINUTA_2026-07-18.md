# Meeting Minutes — Cerro Azul Las Nubes Coffee Farm Project

## 1. Meeting Info
- **Date:** July 18, 2026
- **Time:** 6:30 PM – 9:00 PM
- **Location:** Las Nubes, Huerbsch residence, Cerro Azul
- **Attendees:** Sherry, Bob, Danny, Nathy
- **Note-taker:** Danny

## 2. Purpose/Context
Working session to plan out the farm's operations and business roadmap — varietals, planting timeline, farm management practices, biochar, and labor — plus a bigger-picture conversation about scaling Las Nubes into a self-sustaining operation, including brand and business plan work Danny will lead.

## 3. Key Discussion Points

### Varietals & Nursery (Semillero)
Farm currently only has Catuai: 20–30 plants at 10 years old, 2,500 plants at 3–4 years old. Typica mejorada came up as a variety of interest but isn't on the farm yet. Sherry's point: seed source plants should be 7 years old, in their prime — not young ones.

Plan is 3 semilleros for Catuai, staggered monthly: October, November, December. First seedlings go in the ground in May, then monthly after that. Seed to plantón takes 6–9 months. Estimating 5,000–6,000 seeds total.

Still need to calendarize the semillero tasks for Catuai, and look into sourcing other varietals — not just Boquete, also El Salvador, Costa Rica, Ecuador and elsewhere.

### Planting Goals & Timeline
Goal: 20,000 plantones in 3–4 years, done by 2030, harvesting 2029–2033. Need 6,000 huecos total, 2,000 per lote.

### Farm Map
Need to design a finca map showing the lot divisions.

### Pest & Disease Management
Broca and roya are the main things we're managing. For broca: alcohol traps, 2 gallons needed, 50/50 blend from David, Chiriquí. 30 bottles out in the field now, room to add 10–20 more. Check traps every two weeks. Roya inspection also every two weeks, more often if it's been raining a lot.

Planta por planta weed clearing every 3 months — 2–3 days per lote, 4 lotes, 8 days total for 2,500 plantones.

Ren Ren after harvest — collecting every cherry off the ground and off the plants to keep broca from building up.

### Soil & Foliar Fertilization
Soil: gallinaza + ceniza (from the biochar oven) + shredded leaf litter, applied after harvest with the first rains, right before flowering.

Foliar: 1 part ceniza to 2 parts water, steeped 7 days into lejía. Then 1 liter lejía + 10 liters water + 8 ml melaza per liter — gives N-P-K plus fitosanitario benefit. Two applications a year: first right after flowering (April/May, sometimes Feb depending on rain), second about 8–9 months later.

### Biochar
Applied below the plants and again from about half a meter above — plants are 2m apart so biochar sits between rows. Smaller plantones may get extra applications this year, and all new plantones get it too.

Production per burn: collect fallen wood (1 day, 3 peones + pickup), split it (1 day, 3 peones), load (2 hrs), burn 15 min to light then 20 hrs to burn/cool, shovel into a 55-gal drum while still hot (2 peones), cool 24 hrs outside, bag it (1 hr, 2 peones). Can do a new batch every 3 days.

### Gallinaza & Noria (Compost)
Buy gallinaza twice a year, 300 sacks at 100 lb each, cost $400 USD, aged a year to cut the acidity. Leaf litter gets collected and shredded throughout the summer. Once it's collected and bagged, a worker can shred 30 bags in one day (1 day, 1 peon).

Noria mix: fill with water to 24cm (1 hr, 2 peones), add 30 sacks gallinaza + 30 sacks shredded leaf (by volume), cover with a lona for 5–6 days, then add 20 sacks shredded biochar and let it sit 2–3 more days. Each batch yields around 150 x 5-gallon buckets (filled about 3/4 full, ~4 gallons each), enough for 150 plants.

### Planting Holes (Huecos)
Disinfect holes about a month before the plantón goes in — cal agrícola preferred, ceniza as backup, lining the bottom and walls. Depending on whether we plant the coffee at 6 or 9 months of age, we'd know by the plantón's fifth month whether it's time to start digging and disinfecting the holes — depends on the size and vigor of the plants.

At 60 huecos/day with 2 peones (David + helper), 6,000 huecos takes about 4 months. If we go to 4 peones / 2 crews, that's ~100/day and cuts it to about 2 months — still deciding which way to go.

### Farm Maintenance
Guita clearing lot by lot every 1.5 months — weed wacking the entire farm takes an average of 7 days, depending on weather conditions. Includes areas not yet planted but slated for future years.

### Shade Management
If needed, during the dry season right after the last harvest, before the first rains — late March to mid-April. Pruning or thinning shade as needed.

### Land Clearing (Socoleo)
About 3 hectares to clear, 2 weeks per hectare with 3–4 peones.

### Adjacent Lot — Inversiones Melissa, SA
Danny's been talking to Melissa Grajales and her brother about the 7-hectare lot next door. Offered $10,000, would go up to $50,000 if needed, ideally landing around $20,000. If it happens, plan would be another ~$50,000 to clear the lot and plant 15,000 Catuai over the following years. Not clear yet if they even want to sell — not a priority right now, just laying the groundwork in case it becomes one. Waiting to hear back on a meeting.

### Business Vision
Sherry and Bob want to get the farm to a point where it's self-sustaining — pays for itself, pays the workers, and eventually a salary for Chris and Danny to run and commercialize it. Danny proposed taking this on: a business plan and roadmap, a brand and marketing plan to tell Bob and Sherry's Las Nubes story, an educational/discovery kit under Néctar Nómada to help sell the coffee, obtaining varietals for experimental lots, networking with other farms for capacity building and best practices for micro specialty coffee farming, a software tool for Sherry and Bob to manage pre/post-harvest tasks and reporting, and documenting all the processing, experiments, roasting profiles, and cupping.

### Brand — Finca Rosina
Sherry pre-approved the name Finca Rosina (over Rosina Estates and Hacienda Rosina). Visual direction: their Cerro Azul home and the surrounding landscape, lavender palette.

There's an AI-generated label mockup being used as a reference to hand off to an illustrator. Direction for that illustrator: keep the same composition, hierarchy and style as the approved reference. Matte lavender background, premium editorial feel. "Finca Rosina" as the main name, with origin and altitude below it. Central illustration in linear engraving style — main house, coffee fields, mountains — with coffee branches, flowers, cherries and a hummingbird worked in subtly. Technical info (variety, process, cupping notes, coffee data) in clean blocks. Elegant serif type, lots of white space, balanced layout. No photos, no 3D, nothing cartoonish. Overall feel: botanical plate / expedition notebook — refined, timeless, high-end.

### Beekeeping — Apiary Setup
These are two separate locations, both under Danny's management as distinct apiaries at Las Nubes — small enough in hive count for 1–2 people to manage. Danny will be responsible, operationally and financially, for the gear and equipment used at both sites.

**Apiary 1 (new):** Agreed to place 10 beehives on the property and train Kenneth to support and work on this project alongside Danny. Site inspection is scheduled for Monday, July 20th — the old path toward the river on the Huerbsch side, kept a safe distance from the waterhole. Danny will be responsible for and oversee the project.

While Danny is in Boquete over the next couple of weeks, Kenneth will get 2–3 hours of training and support to build the hives using local wood from Las Nubes. Once the hives are ready, the queen bees and nucleus colonies will be transported and installed. Danny will also leave both oral and injectable medication on-site for minor to major allergic reaction cases.

**Apiary 2 (existing):** There are also 2 existing empty beehives on-site, along the fence line by the road bordering the Grajales property. These will be left as-is in case a wild swarm moves in on its own. Once the road work is finished, that area can be socoleado (cleared) to open up access — Danny will cover this cost if needed — with an eye toward placing additional hives there in the near future or by next season.
