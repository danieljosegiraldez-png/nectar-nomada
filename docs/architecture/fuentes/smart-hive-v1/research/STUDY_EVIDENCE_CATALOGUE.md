# Coffee and cacao: study evidence catalogue

Version 1.0 — 2026-09-16. **40 records**: 16 coffee, 15 cacao, one cross-crop field record, four syntheses/reviews and four methods records. Dataset and morphology papers are included; this is not 40 independent yield trials.

This is a targeted, source-linked evidence map, not a preregistered systematic review or meta-analysis. `abstract` means conclusions were limited to the accessible abstract. `article sections` means indexed sections were available, not that every supplementary file was extracted. Exact instrument SKUs and statistical model specifications are left unextracted when unavailable; do not invent them. Published methods are descriptions, not procurement endorsements. Project lessons below are our interpretation.

Each record has a stable ID also present in study_registry.json. Missing analysis details mean **not extracted**, not that the paper omitted analysis. Accessed September 16, 2026.

## C01 — Roubik (2002)

**The value of bees to the coffee harvest**. [Source](https://stri-sites.si.edu/docs/publications/pdfs/Roubik_coffeepollination.pdf). DOI: 10.1038/417708a.

- **Access:** article sections.
- **Method / practical experience:** Panama; 50 two-year-old arabica plants; open and fine-mesh-bagged control branches.
- **Reported result:** Open branches produced 49% more ripe berries per flower and mean berries were 7% heavier.
- **Reported analysis:** Paired t-test reported for berry abundance.
- **Interpretation for our project:** Use matched branch cohorts and mature harvest outcomes; this does not isolate our stingless colony.

## C02 — Klein, Steffan-Dewenter & Tscharntke (2003)

**Bee pollination and fruit set of Coffea arabica and C. canephora (Rubiaceae)**. [Source](https://bsapubs.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.3732/ajb.90.1.153). DOI: 10.3732/ajb.90.1.153.

- **Access:** article sections.
- **Method / practical experience:** 24 Indonesian agroforestry fields; insect/wind/self and hand-cross comparisons across both crop species.
- **Reported result:** Canephora fruit set: open 78.1%, hand cross 83.8%, wind plus self 62.3%; open and hand cross were not significantly different.
- **Reported analysis:** Not extracted from the accessible material; consult methods/supplement before reproducing.
- **Interpretation for our project:** Store treatment mechanism and species; 78.1 minus 62.3 is 15.8 percentage points, not 15.8% relative gain.

## C03 — Klein, Steffan-Dewenter & Tscharntke (2003)

**Fruit set of highland coffee increases with the diversity of pollinating bees**. [Source](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12803911/). DOI: 10.1098/rspb.2002.2306.

- **Access:** abstract.
- **Method / practical experience:** 24 Indonesian arabica agroforestry systems plus single-visit experiments.
- **Reported result:** Fruit set ranged roughly 60–90% across a bee-richness gradient of 3–20 species; some rare solitary visitors outperformed abundant social species per visit.
- **Reported analysis:** Not extracted from the accessible material; consult methods/supplement before reproducing.
- **Interpretation for our project:** Do not replace functional performance with total bee counts; landscape richness relationships are not randomized richness treatments.

## C04 — Klein, Steffan-Dewenter & Tscharntke (2003)

**Pollination of Coffea canephora in relation to local and regional agroforestry management**. [Source](https://besjournals.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1046/j.1365-2664.2003.00847.x). DOI: 10.1046/j.1365-2664.2003.00847.x.

- **Access:** abstract.
- **Method / practical experience:** 15 Sulawesi agroforestry systems; bee surveys, bagged/open treatments and pollination efficiency of 15 bee species.
- **Reported result:** Open fruit set increased with bee diversity/abundance; solitary assemblages provided higher per-visit fruit set than social assemblages.
- **Reported analysis:** Not extracted from the accessible material; consult methods/supplement before reproducing.
- **Interpretation for our project:** Record habitat, visitor identity and visitation effort; check overlap with other Klein datasets before synthesis.

## C05 — Ricketts et al. (2004)

**Economic value of tropical forest to coffee production**. [Source](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.0405147101). DOI: 10.1073/pnas.0405147101.

- **Access:** abstract.
- **Method / practical experience:** Costa Rica; pollination experiments along replicated forest-distance gradients.
- **Reported result:** Estimated forest-linked yield benefit 20% within about 1 km; peaberry frequency reduced 27%; historical valuation about USD60,000/year for one farm.
- **Reported analysis:** Not extracted from the accessible material; consult methods/supplement before reproducing.
- **Interpretation for our project:** Retain distance, seed form and valuation assumptions; these historical dollar estimates are not our projected revenue.

## C06 — Vergara & Badano (2009)

**Pollinator diversity increases fruit production in Mexican coffee plantations: The importance of rustic management systems**. [Source](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S016788090800234X). DOI: 10.1016/j.agee.2008.08.001.

- **Access:** abstract.
- **Method / practical experience:** Central Veracruz management gradient; observational and experimental measurements of diversity and production.
- **Reported result:** Lower-impact systems had greater visitor richness/diversity and higher fruit production; positive diversity-production associations were reported.
- **Reported analysis:** Not extracted from the accessible material; consult methods/supplement before reproducing.
- **Interpretation for our project:** Management and landscape covariates need separate fields; do not assume their observational association identifies one causal mechanism.

## C07 — Krishnan et al. (2012)

**Status of pollinators and their efficiency in coffee fruit set in a fragmented landscape mosaic in South India**. [Source](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1439179112000291). DOI: 10.1016/j.baae.2012.03.007.

- **Access:** article sections.
- **Method / practical experience:** Kodagu robusta; self, no-pollination, wind, open and hand-cross treatments; visitor observations.
- **Reported result:** Final fruit set: cross 44%, open 33%, wind 22.1%; five-week initial set was unreliable for final set.
- **Reported analysis:** Not extracted from the accessible material; consult methods/supplement before reproducing.
- **Interpretation for our project:** Keep initial and final outcomes separate and include positive hand-cross comparisons when measuring deficits.

## C08 — Boreux et al. (2013)

**Interactive effects among ecosystem services and management practices on crop production: Pollination in coffee agroforestry systems**. [Source](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3666681/). DOI: 10.1073/pnas.1210590110.

- **Access:** article sections.
- **Method / practical experience:** Indian coffee systems; bee abundance, irrigation-induced flowering, shade, lime and environmental variables considered together.
- **Reported result:** Bee abundance benefited production after accounting for management; irrigation and liming effects could exceed shade-cover effects.
- **Reported analysis:** Not extracted from the accessible material; consult methods/supplement before reproducing.
- **Interpretation for our project:** Log irrigation and flowering synchrony; cameras at a busy flowering patch cannot alone identify a hive effect.

## C09 — Classen et al. (2014)

**Complementary ecosystem services provided by pest predators and pollinators increase quantity and quality of coffee yields**. [Source](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3924087/). DOI: 10.1098/rspb.2013.3148.

- **Access:** article sections.
- **Method / practical experience:** 180 coffee bushes, Kilimanjaro land-use gradient; vertebrate × pollinator exclusions. Gauze apertures 0.39 × 0.77 mm; strings kept fabric away from flowers.
- **Reported result:** Pollinators did not significantly change fruit set but increased fruit weight 7.4%; vertebrate exclusion reduced set about 9%.
- **Reported analysis:** Binomial responses for set/retention; mixed-effects analyses with hierarchical units.
- **Interpretation for our project:** Measure mass as well as set; a published coffee mesh cannot be transferred to cacao midges without qualification.

## C10 — Saturni, Jaffé & Metzger (2016)

**Landscape structure influences bee community and coffee pollination at different spatial scales**. [Source](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S016788091630500X). DOI: 10.1016/j.agee.2016.10.008.

- **Access:** article sections.
- **Method / practical experience:** Brazil; nine landscapes, 15 arabica shrubs each, floral exclusions and visitor surveys; 300 m, 1 km and 2 km landscape scales.
- **Reported result:** Authors reported 28% greater fruit set with bees; community relationships differed by spatial scale.
- **Reported analysis:** Not extracted from the accessible material; consult methods/supplement before reproducing.
- **Interpretation for our project:** Store buffer radius with every landscape metric; do not average scales or interpret this reported percentage as percentage points.

## C11 — Hipólito et al. (2020)

**Performance, Effectiveness, and Efficiency of Honeybees as Pollinators of Coffea arabica (Gentianales, Rubiaceae)**. [Source](https://ri-api.inpa.gov.br/server/api/core/bitstreams/4b7095dd-4531-4088-b2de-84b8acf86094/content). DOI: 10.1007/s13744-020-00785-8.

- **Access:** abstract.
- **Method / practical experience:** Bahia; open, wind, hand cross and single-visit bee treatments; pollen deposition, pollen tubes and fruit formation.
- **Reported result:** Single visits transferred pollen; deposition quantity did not differ among treatments, while hand cross and single visits showed greater fertilization measures.
- **Reported analysis:** Not extracted from the accessible material; consult methods/supplement before reproducing.
- **Interpretation for our project:** A counted pollen grain and a fertilizing pollen tube are different endpoints; optional microscopy requires a separate sampling cohort.

## C12 — Martínez-Salinas et al. (2022)

**Interacting pest control and pollination services in coffee systems**. [Source](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9169773/). DOI: 10.1073/pnas.2119959119.

- **Access:** article sections.
- **Method / practical experience:** 30 Costa Rican farms; eight plants/farm; bird exclusion using 20 mm mesh and bee exclusion using 1 mm nylon branch bags installed before bloom and removed after.
- **Reported result:** Joint bird/bee exclusion reduced estimated yield 24.7%; positive interactions affected set, mass and uniformity.
- **Reported analysis:** Models tested bird × bee treatments and fruit condition/time; hierarchical design.
- **Interpretation for our project:** Support nested factorial designs and interaction terms; this is a combined-services result, not a bee-only yield estimate.

## C13 — Aristizábal et al. (2025)

**Bee pollination affects coffee quality, yield, and trade-offs within them**. [Source](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0167880924003761). DOI: 10.1016/j.agee.2024.109258.

- **Access:** abstract.
- **Method / practical experience:** 30 plants in Tarrazú, Costa Rica; bee exclusions, harvest measurements and certified cuppers; online 2024, journal volume 2025.
- **Reported result:** Authors reported fruit set +9%, aroma +2%, berry mass −7%, with body/balance trade-offs.
- **Reported analysis:** Not extracted from the accessible material; consult methods/supplement before reproducing.
- **Interpretation for our project:** Archive processing and blind cupping batches; improved set does not imply every quality attribute improves.

## C14 — Almeida-Dias et al. (2025)

**Managed Africanized honey bees and native stingless bees increase Arabica coffee yields in southeastern Brazil**. [Source](https://www.embrapa.br/en/busca-de-publicacoes/-/publicacao/1176761/managed-africanized-honey-bees-and-native-stingless-bees-increase-arabica-coffee-yields-in-southeastern-brazil). DOI: 10.1590/1678-992X-2023-0049.

- **Access:** abstract.
- **Method / practical experience:** Managed Apis and Scaptotrigona introduced in 23 coffee fields; nearby versus more distant comparisons.
- **Reported result:** Yield per bush increased 16% near colonies; stingless-bee effects had higher variability with low replication.
- **Reported analysis:** Not extracted from the accessible material; consult methods/supplement before reproducing.
- **Interpretation for our project:** Useful colony-addition precedent; identify spillover and background colony confounding. Check dataset overlap with C15.

## C15 — Ramos et al. (2026)

**Stingless bees in coffee: yield gains and assessing neonicotinoid impact**. [Source](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/bee-science/articles/10.3389/frbee.2025.1644205/full). DOI: 10.3389/frbee.2025.1644205.

- **Access:** article sections.
- **Method / practical experience:** Brazil; Scaptotrigona depilis, conventional/organic farms; near <50 m versus 200–300 m; 360 harvested branches across six yield-study farms.
- **Reported result:** Near-colony branches yielded 67% more; measured colony endpoints did not show significant adverse effects under the studied exposure.
- **Reported analysis:** Not extracted from the accessible material; consult methods/supplement before reproducing.
- **Interpretation for our project:** Do not infer universal pesticide safety or guaranteed gains; distinguish branch outcomes from C14 bush outcomes. Published January 6, 2026 despite the 2025 DOI suffix.

## C16 — Narayanan, Krishnan & Osuri (2026)

**Nocturnal insect communities altered by land-use change contribute little to coffee pollination in the Western Ghats, India**. ## K01 — Valle, de Almeida & Leite (1990)

**Energy costs of flowering, fruiting, and cherelle wilt in cacao**. [Source](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/14972943/). DOI: 10.1093/treephys/6.3.329.

- **Access:** abstract.
- **Method / practical experience:** Fourteen-year-old trees: hand pollination, natural pollination and fruit removal; 1983–1986 tracking of flowers, cherelles, pods and biochemical composition.
- **Reported result:** Hand pollination produced more cherelles but not significantly more mature fruits because wilting increased.
- **Reported analysis:** Not extracted from the accessible material; consult methods/supplement before reproducing.
- **Interpretation for our project:** A required counterexample to early-set yield claims; maintain competing fruit-loss outcomes.

## K02 — Falque et al. (1995)

**Effect of pollination intensity on fruit and seed set in cacao (Theobroma cacao L.)**. [Source](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/BF00243203). DOI: 10.1007/BF00243203.

- **Access:** abstract.
- **Method / practical experience:** Ivory Coast, clone UPA409; deposited pollen related to pod survival and seeds; styles counted after cutting 24 hours post-pollination.
- **Reported result:** Dose-response and saturation in fruit/seed outcomes; published thresholds were specific to the tested material.
- **Reported analysis:** Not extracted from the accessible material; consult methods/supplement before reproducing.
- **Interpretation for our project:** Do not encode a universal minimum pollen count; destructive style sampling needs a validated crop/genotype procedure.

## K03 — Groeneveld et al. (2010)

**Experimental evidence for stronger cacao yield limitation by pollination than by plant resources**. [Source](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1433831910000211). DOI: 10.1016/j.ppees.2010.02.005.

- **Access:** article sections.
- **Method / practical experience:** Sulawesi; 10/40/70/100% hand pollination of accessible flowers up to 2 m, crossed with resource treatments in two experiments.
- **Reported result:** Yield rose nonlinearly to about twice current levels; largest change was 10→40%; tested resource effects were nonsignificant.
- **Reported analysis:** Not extracted from the accessible material; consult methods/supplement before reproducing.
- **Interpretation for our project:** Accessible-flower denominator matters; twice baseline is +100%, not +200%, and is not a universal optimum.

## K04 — Forbes & Northfield (2017)

**Increased pollinator habitat enhances cacao fruit set and predator conservation**. [Source](https://esajournals.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/eap.1491). DOI: 10.1002/eap.1491.

- **Access:** abstract.
- **Method / practical experience:** Cacao husk additions crossed with pollen supplementation; pollination and predator responses measured.
- **Reported result:** Husks increased fruits per tree; supplementation/habitat treatments improved final yield, while spider/skink densities also increased.
- **Reported analysis:** Not extracted from the accessible material; consult methods/supplement before reproducing.
- **Interpretation for our project:** Habitat manipulation is a distinct treatment; source/disease status, substrate mass and maintenance must be logged. Online 2016, issue 2017.

## K05 — Forbes et al. (2019)

**Supplemental and synchronized pollination may increase yield in cacao**. [Source](https://researchonline.jcu.edu.au/60921/). DOI: 10.21273/HORTSCI12852-18.

- **Access:** abstract.
- **Method / practical experience:** Three high-yielding clones; donor genotype, pollination intensity and synchrony manipulated.
- **Reported result:** Supplementation improved yield; donor and cherelle age mattered; pollination intensity did not significantly change harvested yield.
- **Reported analysis:** Not extracted from the accessible material; consult methods/supplement before reproducing.
- **Interpretation for our project:** Track donor-recipient pair, cohort ages and intensity independently; increasing labor does not guarantee increased harvest.

## K06 — Toledo-Hernández et al. (2020)

**Hand pollination, not pesticides or fertilizers, increases cocoa yields and farmer income**. [Source](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0167880920303467). DOI: 10.1016/j.agee.2020.107160.

- **Access:** article sections.
- **Method / practical experience:** Sulawesi; partial trial used 128 trees, accessible flowers over 60 days; pollination compared with agrochemical inputs, plus full-pollination trial.
- **Reported result:** Reported yield increases 51% for 13% partial pollination and 161% for full pollination; study-specific net-income increase about 69%.
- **Reported analysis:** Not extracted from the accessible material; consult methods/supplement before reproducing.
- **Interpretation for our project:** Record labor/opportunity costs and intervention coverage. These are separate treatment comparisons, not one universal response curve.

## K07 — Vanhove et al. (2020)

**Impact of insecticide and pollinator-enhancing substrate applications on cocoa (Theobroma cacao) cherelle and pod production in Côte d’Ivoire**. [Source](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0167880920300402). DOI: 10.1016/j.agee.2020.106855.

- **Access:** article sections.
- **Method / practical experience:** Three farms; banana pseudostems with/without insecticide and distance comparisons; 25-week observation period.
- **Reported result:** Some treatments increased cherelles, but accumulated mature pod numbers did not significantly increase.
- **Reported analysis:** Generalized mixed models.
- **Interpretation for our project:** Do not convert substrate-related early set into yield or recommend insecticide from this result.

## K08 — Vansynghel et al. (2022)

**Cacao flower visitation: Low pollen deposition, low fruit set and dominance of herbivores**. [Source](https://besjournals.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/2688-8319.12140). DOI: 10.1002/2688-8319.12140.

- **Access:** article sections.
- **Method / practical experience:** Peruvian cacao; sticky-glue flower visitor sampling, pollen assessment and separate hand-pollination comparison.
- **Reported result:** Natural set 39/1952 (~2%); hand pollination 70/968 (~7%), assessed as healthy cherelles six days later; visitor composition differed regionally.
- **Reported analysis:** Binomial GLMM for pollination comparison.
- **Interpretation for our project:** Trap counts, video visits and six-day set are distinct measures; do not use sticky-treated flowers as undisturbed yield cohorts.

## K09 — Vansynghel et al. (2022)

**Quantifying services and disservices provided by insects and vertebrates in cacao agroforestry landscapes**. [Source](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9470269/). DOI: 10.1098/rspb.2022.1309.

- **Access:** article sections.
- **Method / practical experience:** Northwestern Peru, 12 agroforests; flying insects, ants, birds and bats excluded in factorial combinations, about one year follow-up.
- **Reported result:** Open set averaged 1.7%, flying-insect exclusion 0.3%; bird/bat access more than doubled yield; fruit predation caused additional losses.
- **Reported analysis:** Not extracted from the accessible material; consult methods/supplement before reproducing.
- **Interpretation for our project:** Maintain separate access factors and loss causes. One exclusion bag is not a taxon-specific experiment unless its permeability is verified.

## K10 — Vansynghel et al. (2023)

**Cross-pollination with native genotypes improves fruit set and yield quality of Peruvian cacao**. [Source](https://ucrisportal.univie.ac.at/en/publications/cross-pollination-with-native-genotypes-improves-fruit-set-and-yi/). DOI: 10.1016/j.agee.2023.108671.

- **Access:** abstract.
- **Method / practical experience:** Self-pollen versus five native donor genotypes; microclimate and seed outcomes considered.
- **Reported result:** Self-pollination success 0.5%; cross-pollination 3–8 times higher; donor identity mattered and supplemented fruits had higher seed weight/premium content.
- **Reported analysis:** Not extracted from the accessible material; consult methods/supplement before reproducing.
- **Interpretation for our project:** Store compatibility and donor identity rather than only hand_pollinated=true; do not interpret 3–8-fold as high absolute success.

## K11 — Lander et al. (2025)

**Global chocolate supply is limited by low pollination and high temperatures**. [Source](https://www.nature.com/articles/s43247-025-02072-z). DOI: 10.1038/s43247-025-02072-z.

- **Reported result:** Reported hand-pollination yield benefit 20%; temperature and tree/soil-related covariates also explained variation.
- **Reported analysis:** Not extracted from the accessible material; consult methods/supplement before reproducing.

## K12 — Merle et al. (2025)

**Not all flowers' visitors are pollinators: Combining indicators to identify their role in the cocoa pollination service**. [Source](https://publications.cirad.fr/une_notice.php?dk=614057). DOI: 10.1016/j.agee.2025.109716.

- **Access:** abstract.
- **Method / practical experience:** Controlled pollen quantity/quality trial plus transects/video in Ecuador and indicators of visitor contribution.
- **Reported result:** Pollen quantity drove fruit-set/ovule responses; crawling arthropods including ants/aphids were identified as promising contributors in that context.
- **Reported analysis:** Not extracted from the accessible material; consult methods/supplement before reproducing.
- **Interpretation for our project:** Store evidence per claim, region and method; do not collapse this finding and K13 into a universal visitor whitelist.

## K13 — Van de Sande et al. (2026)

**Revisiting cacao pollination: new behavioural and pollen-based evidence for the key role of Ceratopogonidae**. [Source](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S143917912600037X). DOI: 10.1016/j.baae.2026.05.006.

- **Access:** article sections.
- **Method / practical experience:** Malaysia/French Guiana; randomized 10-minute sessions, aspirator sampling, microclimate, microscopy and subset barcoding. Pollen checked fresh before ethanol.
- **Reported result:** Forcipomyia were strongest candidates; 43% of Malaysian biting midges carried cacao pollen. Visit-specific fertilization was not directly measured.
- **Reported analysis:** Chi-square behavioural comparisons; Welch temperature and rank-based humidity comparisons.
- **Interpretation for our project:** Preserve specimen processing order and distinction between compatible behaviour and demonstrated effectiveness.

## K14 — Wolcott et al. (2023)

**3D pollination biology using micro-computed tomography and geometric morphometrics in Theobroma cacao**. [Source](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10617321/). DOI: 10.1002/aps3.11549.

- **Access:** article sections.
- **Method / practical experience:** Micro-CT of flowers and potential visitors; landmark geometry with 3D Slicer and geomorph; floral entry dimensions compared with insect bodies.
- **Reported result:** Floral access geometry separated potential visitors into differing morphological suitability classes.
- **Reported analysis:** Geometric morphometric analysis; body/floral dimension comparison.
- **Interpretation for our project:** Microscopy/CT can support hypotheses but is not a visit-to-fruit experiment; outsource specialist imaging instead of buying CT for the pilot.

## K15 — Xu et al. (2025)

**Identifying Cocoa Flower Visitors: A Deep Learning Dataset**. [Source](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41597-025-05631-3). DOI: 10.1038/s41597-025-05631-3.

- **Access:** abstract.
- **Method / practical experience:** Hainan embedded-camera data; 5,792 insect images and 1,082 background images curated from 23 million images; five visitor groups.
- **Reported result:** Best reported tested configuration: YOLOv8 medium, F1 0.71 and mAP50 0.70.
- **Reported analysis:** Not extracted from the accessible material; consult methods/supplement before reproducing.
- **Interpretation for our project:** Use published 2025 version, not 2024 preprint as a second study. Family/group labels and detection metrics are not species accuracy or proof of pollination.

## X01 — Bos et al. (2007)
