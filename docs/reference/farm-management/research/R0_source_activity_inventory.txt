Managing a coffee plantation requires a rigorous, year-round cycle of field management, agronomy, manual labor, and processing operations. Because coffee is grown across different hemispheres and altitudes, the precise calendar months vary (e.g., Central America harvests from November to March, while East Africa and parts of South America differ). However, the core operational progression remains uniform. [1, 2, 3, 4] 
Below is the complete inventory of coffee farmer and operator activities throughout the year, categorized by Functional Class and sub-categorized by Activity Type.
------------------------------
## Class 1: Agronomy & Crop Management
These activities focus directly on the health, structure, and physiological cycles of the coffee trees to maximize yield and quality. [1, 5] 
## 1. Propagation & Nursery Operations (Pre-Planting Cycle)

* Substrate Preparation: Mixing topsoil with organic compost/manure and sun-exposing the mixture to eliminate soil-borne pathogens.
* Seed Bed Sowing: Sowing certified coffee seeds (e.g., Arabica or Robusta varieties) into controlled beds.
* Seedling Replanting (Potted Transfer): Transferring sprouted seedlings (in the "soldier" or "butterfly" stage) into polyethylene nursery bags.
* Nursery Maintenance: Routine watering, weeding, and shade monitoring of young seedlings for 6–9 months.
* Culling / Selection: Identifying and discarding weak or root-deformed seedlings prior to field transport. [5, 6, 7] 

## 2. Plantation Establishment & Renewal

* Site Selection & Mapping: Assessing altitude, soil pH (ideal: 5.5–6.5), and slope gradient.
* Holing / Pit Digging: Digging systematic planting holes (spaced ~2.5m for Arabica, ~3m for Robusta) months before the rainy season to allow soil aeration.
* Transplanting: Planting selection-grade nursery saplings into the field at the onset of the rainy season.
* Infilling (Re-planting): Replacing dead, diseased, or stunted young trees in established plots. [1, 5, 7, 8, 9] 

## 3. Canopy & Architectural Management (Post-Harvest Phase)

* Pruning & Stumping: Cutting away old, unproductive primary branches and stumping senescent trees to stimulate new vegetative growth.
* Desuckering / Thinning: Periodically removing unwanted water shoots (suckers) from the main trunk to channel energy into the main fruiting branches.
* Shade Tree Regulation: Lopping and thinning canopy shade trees to maintain an optimal balance of sunlight filter (approx. 40-50% shade).
* Bending / Agrobiolo Guiding: Bending vertical branches on young trees to trigger lateral branching. [1, 3, 5, 8, 9, 10] 

------------------------------
## Class 2: Soil Health & Integrated Pest Management (IPM)
These activities maintain nutrition, protect the topsoil ecosystem, and combat economic pests and diseases. [1, 2] 
## 1. Nutrition & Soil Amendments

* Soil Sampling: Collecting core soil samples across different farm zones for laboratory chemical analysis.
* Macro-Nutrient Application: Ground application of granular NPK (Nitrogen, Phosphorus, Potassium) or organic manure at specific physiological windows (pre-flowering, fruit-set).
* Foliar Feeding: Spraying liquid micro-nutrients (Zinc, Boron) directly onto the leaves to optimize coffee flowering and fruit development.
* Liming: Applying agricultural lime or dolomite to correct acidic soil pH. [1, 5, 9] 

## 2. Weed & Ecosystem Management

* Manual Weeding / Slashing: Using machetes or hoes to clear competing undergrowth around the base of the coffee trees.
* Mulching: Covering the root zones with pruned branches, organic matter, or recycled coffee pulp to preserve moisture and suppress weeds.
* Terracing & Cover Cropping: Establishing contour ridges, grass strips, or terracing to mitigate soil erosion on hillside plots. [3, 5, 8, 10, 11] 

## 3. Disease & Pest Control

* Phytosanitary Monitoring: Routine scouting for infestations like the Coffee Berry Borer (Broca), Coffee Leaf Rust (CLR), and Coffee Berry Disease (CBD).
* Fungicide / Insecticide Application: Applying targeted organic or chemical controls before and during fruit development.
* Trapping & Cultural Control: Placing alcohol traps for borers and gathering fallen, mummified berries from the ground to interrupt pest lifecycles. [1, 5, 8, 9] 

------------------------------
## Class 3: Harvesting & Post-Harvest Operations
The most labor-intensive portion of the cycle, requiring strict timeline execution to preserve cup quality. [11, 12] 
## 1. Harvesting Operations

* Pre-Harvest Prep: Clearing paths between rows and cleaning collection baskets, bags, or tarps.
* Selective Picking: Hand-picking only perfectly uniform, bright-red ripe cherries in multiple rounds over a 2–4 month window.
* Strip Picking: Stripping all remaining cherries (green, ripe, overripe) from branches during the final pass of the harvest season. [1, 11, 13] 

## 2. Wet/Dry Primary Processing

* Flotation Sorting: Submerging harvested cherries in water tanks to separate lightweight "floaters" (defective, insect-damaged) from dense, high-quality "sinkers".
* De-pulping: Mechanically stripping the outer skin and pulp from the bean within 24 hours of harvest.
* Fermentation: Storing mucilage-coated beans in tanks for 12–36 hours to break down sugars.
* Washing & Soaking: Washing the fermented beans with clean water to remove remaining mucilage.
* Pulp Recycling: Transporting wet coffee pulp away from processing sites to composting pits for reuse as fertilizer. [11, 14] 

## 3. Drying & Conditioning

* Patio/Table Raking: Spreading parchment coffee on concrete patios or raised African drying beds.
* Constant Turning: Manually raking and turning the beans every hour to ensure uniform drying and prevent mold development.
* Moisture Testing: Monitoring drying progress until the internal moisture content stabilizes precisely between 10% and 12%.
* Bagging & Conditioning: Packing dried parchment into clean jute or sisal sacks and storing them in ventilated warehouses to rest (condition). [11, 15] 

------------------------------
## Class 4: Farm Infrastructure & Business Management
The back-office and structural work required to keep the farm legally, financially, and physically operational. [9, 16] 
## 1. Asset & Infrastructure Maintenance

* Equipment Servicing: Cleaning, sharpening, and calibrating pulping machines, depalpers, solar dryers, and moisture meters ahead of harvest.
* Water-System Management: Managing drainage lines, sediment ponds, and wastewater filtration setups to avoid environmental contamination. [1, 6, 11] 

## 2. Administration & Financial Compliance

* Labor Management: Recruiting, onboarding, housing, and tracking payroll for seasonal pickers.
* Traceability & Record Keeping: Logging harvest volumes, lot tracking codes, chemical application records, and cooperative transaction files.
* Financial Planning & Budgeting: Evaluating cash flow, managing credit lines, and tracking the cost of inputs (fertilizers, bags) against seasonal coffee sales. [9, 16, 17] 

------------------------------
## High-Level Structural Comparison

| Functional Class | Frequency / Seasonality | Main Objective | Labor Intensity |
|---|---|---|---|
| Agronomy & Crop Management | Cyclical (Post-Harvest & Rainy Season) | Structural growth and tree rejuvenation | Medium to High |
| Soil Health & IPM | Regular intervals throughout the year | Plant nutrition and biological protection | Medium |
| Harvest & Post-Harvest | High season (Varies by geography) | Quality preservation and value extraction | Critical / Very High |
| Infrastructure & Business | Continuous / Daily year-round | Compliance, upkeep, and financial health | Low to Medium |
